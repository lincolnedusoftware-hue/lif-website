What is ACCA FIA? A Beginner's Guide for Students

By Ammar Ali · Updated 6 September 2026

ACCA FIA, or Foundations in Accountancy, is ACCA’s entry-level route into accounting, built for people with no accounting background and no formal qualifications to start. It is a suite of seven short papers that teach you the fundamentals of finance and accounting, and it feeds directly into the full ACCA qualification with exemptions for the work you have already done (ACCA Global). If you have finished Matric or O-Levels and want a globally recognised finance career, FIA is usually where your ACCA journey begins. This guide explains exactly what FIA is, what you study, what you earn, and how it connects to full ACCA.

What is ACCA FIA?

ACCA FIA (Foundations in Accountancy) is a group of entry-level qualifications created by ACCA for students who are new to accounting. There are no academic entry requirements to begin, so you can start straight after school (ACCA Global). You work through seven papers, from the basics of recording transactions up to diploma level, and each stage gives you a certificate you can put on your CV before you have even finished.

The point of FIA is to open the door. It teaches the foundations that the full ACCA qualification assumes you already know, then lets you carry that work forward as exemptions when you move up. Think of it as the on-ramp to ACCA rather than a separate destination. For the full path from here to a qualified accountant, see our complete guide to ACCA in Pakistan.

What are the FIA papers?

FIA is made up of seven papers across three levels. Each level earns its own certificate, so you build recognised qualifications as you go rather than waiting years for one result.

LevelPapersWhat you get
IntroductoryFA1 (Recording Financial Transactions), MA1 (Management Information)ACCA Diploma in Financial and Management Accounting (RQF Level 2)
IntermediateFA2 (Maintaining Financial Records), MA2 (Managing Costs and Finance)ACCA Diploma in Financial and Management Accounting (RQF Level 3)
DiplomaFBT (Business and Technology), FMA (Management Accounting), FFA (Financial Accounting)ACCA Diploma in Accounting and Business (RQF Level 4)

Each award also requires Foundations in Professionalism, a short free ethics module (ACCA Global). The introductory papers assume no prior knowledge, so they are a gentle start. By the diploma level you are covering the same three subjects that open the full ACCA qualification, which is exactly why they carry over as exemptions later (ACCA Global).

Why do the diploma papers have different codes (FBT, FMA, FFA)?

You will see the same three exams written two ways, and it confuses almost every beginner, so here is the plain version. At FIA level the diploma papers are FBT, FMA and FFA. In the full ACCA Qualification the identical three exams are called BT (Business and Technology), MA (Management Accounting) and FA (Financial Accounting), which is the Applied Knowledge stage. Older study material labels the same papers F1, F2 and F3.

So FBT is BT (formerly F1), FMA is MA (formerly F2), and FFA is FA (formerly F3). They are the same exams, just badged for the FIA route. That is the whole reason completing them at FIA level exempts you from Applied Knowledge when you move up: you have already passed those exact papers (ACCA Global).

FIA vs ACCA: what is the difference?

This is the question most beginners have, so here is the plain answer. FIA is the foundation stage. The ACCA Qualification is the full professional qualification that leads to ACCA membership. FIA is not a smaller version of ACCA, it is the first step of the same journey.

ACCA FIA (Foundations)Full ACCA Qualification
Who it is forBeginners, school leavers, no accounting backgroundStudents continuing to full professional status
Entry requirementsNoneTwo A-Levels plus three GCSEs, or entry via FIA
Number of papers7 (across 3 levels)13 exams across Applied Knowledge, Applied Skills and Strategic Professional
What you earnCertificates, then the Diploma in Accounting and Business (RQF Level 4)ACCA membership (once exams and experience are complete)
Leads toExemptions into full ACCAA globally recognised professional accountant

In short, you use FIA to get in, then you use full ACCA to qualify. Completing the FIA diploma level does not just prepare you for ACCA, it removes the first three ACCA exams from your list.

How does FIA lead into the full ACCA qualification?

When you complete the diploma level of FIA (FBT, FMA and FFA), you earn the ACCA Diploma in Accounting and Business at RQF Level 4, and with it you are exempt from ACCA’s first three Applied Knowledge exams: BT, MA and FA (ACCA Global). You transfer onto the full ACCA Qualification and begin at Applied Skills, having already cleared the opening stage.

That is the real value of the route. You are not repeating work, you are banking it: three exams cleared before the full qualification even begins. For the full breakdown, see our guide on ACCA exemptions after FIA.

Who is ACCA FIA for?

FIA is designed for anyone who wants a finance career but does not have an accounting background yet. It suits:

  • School leavers who have finished Matric or O-Levels and want to start early.
  • Students switching fields into accounting from another subject.
  • Working adults who need a flexible, step-by-step route into a recognised qualification.

Because there are no academic entry requirements, the FIA route is genuinely open to beginners (ACCA Global). If you are starting straight after school, our guide on how to begin ACCA after Matric or O-Levels walks through the enrolment steps in order.

How long does ACCA FIA take, and what does it cost?

Most students complete the FIA papers in around 12 to 18 months, depending on how many exams they sit per cycle and their pass rates (ACCA Global). It is a realistic, manageable stage rather than a long haul, and finishing it early sets up the rest of your ACCA journey. For the full qualification timeline, see how long ACCA takes in Pakistan.

Cost comes in two parts. First, ACCA charges its own fees in GBP: a one-time registration fee of £89 and an annual subscription of £140 for 2026, plus an entry fee for each exam you sit (ACCA Global). Second, tuition, study materials and support are paid locally in PKR, and they depend on your intake and study mode. We do not publish a fixed tuition figure here because it changes, so book a free counselling session for an exact, current cost for your situation.

Is ACCA FIA recognised in Pakistan?

Yes, and this reassures both students and parents. Since 2015, the Inter Board Committee of Chairmen (IBCC) has recognised ACCA’s Foundation Diploma as equivalent to the Higher Secondary School Certificate (HSSC), Commerce group (ACCA Global). So the FIA route carries a recognised local equivalence, and it sits inside a qualification recognised in 180 countries by more than 240,000 members worldwide (ACCA Global).

That combination, local recognition plus global standing, is why FIA is a credible first step and not a shortcut with a catch.

Frequently asked questions

What is ACCA FIA in simple terms?

ACCA FIA (Foundations in Accountancy) is ACCA’s entry-level route into accounting. It is a suite of seven papers with no academic entry requirements, designed for beginners, and it leads into the full ACCA qualification with exemptions.

How many papers are in ACCA FIA?

Seven, across three levels: FA1 and MA1 (introductory), FA2 and MA2 (intermediate), and FBT, FMA and FFA (diploma). Completing the diploma level earns the Diploma in Accounting and Business at RQF Level 4.

What is the difference between FIA and ACCA?

FIA is the foundation stage for beginners, while the ACCA Qualification is the full professional qualification that leads to membership. FIA is the first step of the same journey, and it exempts you from ACCA’s first three exams once you finish the diploma level.

Do I need any qualifications to start ACCA FIA?

No. There are no academic entry requirements to begin FIA (ACCA Global), so you can start after Matric or O-Levels, or when switching into accounting from another field.

Is ACCA FIA recognised in Pakistan?

Yes. Since 2015 the IBCC has recognised the ACCA Foundation Diploma as equivalent to the HSSC Commerce group (ACCA Global), and ACCA itself is recognised in 180 countries.
