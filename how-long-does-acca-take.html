How Long Does ACCA Take in Pakistan (and How to Finish Faster)

By Ammar Ali · Updated 14 September 2026

Most students in Pakistan complete the ACCA exams in about three to four years, alongside the 36 months of practical experience that usually overlaps with study rather than following it (ACCA Global). If you start through Foundations in Accountancy (FIA), add roughly 12 to 18 months for the foundation stage. So a school leaver who begins now can realistically be a qualified ACCA member in their early twenties. This guide breaks the timeline down stage by stage, and shows the honest ways to finish faster without cutting corners.

How long does ACCA take in Pakistan?

For a typical student, ACCA takes around three to four years to clear all the exams, then membership follows once you have completed 36 months of relevant work experience and 9 performance objectives (ACCA Global). The good news is that the experience does not have to come after your exams. It can be earned before, during or after, so most students finish the practical requirement close to the time they finish studying.

Your real timeline depends on three things: where you start, how many exams you pass per year, and whether you retake any. Start early and pass first time, and you finish young. For the full picture of the qualification itself, see our complete guide to ACCA in Pakistan.

What are the ACCA stages, and how long does each take?

ACCA is built in stages, and each one has a realistic time range. Here is the honest breakdown for a Pakistani student starting from school.

StageExamsRealistic time
FIA (Foundations)7 papers (FA1, MA1, FA2, MA2, FBT, FMA, FFA)About 12 to 18 months (ACCA Global)
Applied KnowledgeBT, MA, FASkipped if you came through FIA; otherwise a few months (on-demand exams)
Applied SkillsLW, PM, TX, FR, AA, FMAbout 1.5 years
Strategic ProfessionalSBL, SBR + 2 optional papersAbout 1 year
Practical experience (PER)36 months + 9 objectivesRuns alongside your studies, not after (ACCA Global)

If you enter through FIA, remember that the diploma stage exempts you from all three Applied Knowledge exams, so you move straight to Applied Skills and save that step (ACCA Global). Our guide on what ACCA FIA is explains how the foundation route works.

How many ACCA exams can you take per year?

This is the single biggest lever on your timeline. ACCA holds four exam sessions a year, in March, June, September and December. You can enter a maximum of four exams per session and a maximum of eight different exams per calendar year (ACCA Global). The three Applied Knowledge exams (BT, MA, FA) and the FIA papers are on-demand computer-based exams, so you can sit them at almost any time rather than waiting for a session.

In practice, ACCA recommends attempting no more than four exams in any six-month period. Most successful students pass three to four exams a year, which is what turns the “three to four years” estimate into reality.

Does the practical experience add extra time?

Not if you plan it well. ACCA requires 36 months of relevant work experience and 9 performance objectives, but this experience can be gained before, during or after your exams (ACCA Global). That means you can start counting relevant work while you are still sitting papers.

Students who line up a relevant job or internship early often complete both the exams and the experience at roughly the same time. Those who wait until all exams are done before starting work are the ones who add years to the total. If you can, study and work in parallel.

How can you finish ACCA faster?

Speed comes from smart planning, not from rushing. Here is what actually shortens the timeline:

  1. Start early. Begin after Matric or O-Levels through FIA rather than waiting for a degree. Our guide on how to start ACCA after Matric or O-Levels shows the steps.
  2. Use every exemption. The FIA diploma skips all three Applied Knowledge exams. A relevant degree can exempt more. Every exemption is time you do not spend.
  3. Sit on-demand exams immediately. The knowledge and FIA papers are on-demand, so do not wait for a session to take them.
  4. Pass first time. Retakes are the biggest hidden delay. Good tuition and proper preparation beat cramming for four papers at once.
  5. Overlap your work experience. Start relevant work early so the 36 months finishes near your last exam, not years later.
  6. Plan a steady three to four exams a year. Consistent progress beats an ambitious plan that ends in resits.

What can slow ACCA down?

A few common mistakes stretch the timeline out:

  • Retaking exams. Failing a paper pushes it to the next session and costs another fee.
  • Registering late for exams. Late entry costs more and can mean missing a session entirely.
  • Taking too many papers at once. Overloading often leads to resits, which is slower than a steady pace.
  • Delaying work experience. Leaving all 36 months until after the exams adds years to full membership.

ACCA exam dates and registration deadlines

ACCA holds four exam sessions a year, in March, June, September and December, while the Applied Knowledge and FIA exams are on-demand computer-based exams you can book almost any time. ACCA publishes the exact dates and the registration windows for each session on its exam sessions page, so check them early each cycle.

The deadlines matter for your budget as much as your schedule. Early entry is the cheapest, standard entry costs more, and late entry is the most expensive, so registering in good time saves money and protects your plan. Frequently asked questions

How long does ACCA take on average?

For most students, ACCA takes around three to four years to complete the exams, plus 36 months of practical experience that usually overlaps with study (ACCA Global). Starting through FIA adds roughly 12 to 18 months for the foundation stage.

How many ACCA exams can I take in a year?

ACCA runs four exam sessions a year (March, June, September and December). You can sit up to four exams per session and up to eight different exams per calendar year (ACCA Global), though ACCA recommends no more than four in any six-month period.

Can I finish ACCA in 2 years?

It is very difficult from scratch. The exams alone usually take three to four years at a safe pace, and membership also needs 36 months of experience. You can shorten the total with exemptions, on-demand exams and by overlapping work experience with study.

Does practical experience add to the time?

Not if you plan it. The 36 months of experience can be gained before, during or after your exams (ACCA Global), so most students complete it around the same time they finish studying rather than afterward.

How can I finish ACCA faster?

Start early through FIA, claim every exemption, sit on-demand exams without waiting, aim to pass first time, and overlap your work experience with your studies. Steady progress of three to four exams a year is the reliable route.
